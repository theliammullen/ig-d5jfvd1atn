Wealthsimple TFSA

How to do it in Wealthsimple (about 2 minutes)

  1. Open Wealthsimple and pick your TFSA account.
  2. Search the ticker (for example XEQT) and tap Buy.
  3. Choose to buy in dollars (not shares) and type the amount, like $7. Buying a slice of a share like this is called a fractional share.
  4. Repeat for the other two, then come back and tap “I did it”.

Every pick here is priced in Canadian dollars (Canadian-listed ETFs, or CDRs, which are Canadian-dollar versions of big U.S. stocks). That avoids Wealthsimple's 1.5% currency-exchange fee. If you see two versions of a stock, pick the one marked CDR or listed in CAD, never the USD one.

Weekend or evening? That's fine. Your order just waits and goes through when the market opens next.

TFSA tips, in plain English

  • Your growth is tax-free. A TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account) means you never pay tax on the money it earns, even when you take it out.
  • Mind your contribution room. That's the yearly limit on how much you can put in. Going over costs a 1%-per-month penalty. $10 a day is about $3,650 a year, well under the yearly limit, but check your exact room in the CRA “My Account” or in Wealthsimple.
  • Taking money out is OK. You get that room back on January 1 of the next year, not right away.
  • Buy, don't just deposit. Cash sitting in your TFSA doesn't grow. Make sure each $10 actually buys the funds.

This is a simple plan, not financial advice. Markets go down as well as up, and nothing here is guaranteed. Only invest money you won't need soon.

Your holdings

What “cash out value” means

It's what your holdings would sell for at the latest market prices. Inside your TFSA there's no tax when you sell or withdraw, and Wealthsimple charges no commission to sell Canadian-dollar listings like these, so the cash out value equals the market value. After you sell, the trade takes 1 business day to settle before the cash can be withdrawn. Real sale prices can be a little different from the latest quote.

Prices come from a free public source and may be delayed about 15 minutes or briefly unavailable. Always trust the numbers in your Wealthsimple app first. Not financial advice.

How to cash out

When you want to use the money, here's how to get it from your Wealthsimple TFSA to your bank. You can take out any amount, any time.

  1. Sell. In Wealthsimple, open your TFSA, tap each fund (XEQT, CASH, etc.) and tap Sell. You can sell in dollars, just like buying. Do it after 11:00 AM Atlantic on a weekday for the steadiest price.
  2. Wait one business day. Trades take one business day to “settle” (that's the official handover of money, called T+1). Sell on Friday and the cash is ready Monday.
  3. Withdraw. Tap Move money → Withdraw, choose your TFSA and your bank account. The money usually lands in a few business days.
  4. Update this app. Go to Tracker → Update real balances so your numbers stay honest.

Taxes: the good news

  • TFSA withdrawals are tax-free. You don't pay tax on what you take out, including all your growth. You don't even have to report it.
  • Your room comes back, but not right away. Whatever you withdraw is added back to your contribution room on January 1 of next year.

The one trap to avoid

If you take money out and then put it back in the same calendar year, that re-deposit uses up new room. If you don't have enough room left, the CRA charges a 1% per month penalty on the extra. Simple rule: if you withdraw this year, wait until January to put that money back (your normal $10 a day is fine as long as you have room left; check it in CRA My Account).

Before you sell

Selling right after a big drop locks in the loss. If you don't need the money, staying put has historically been the better move. Your CASH holding is the best part to take out first for small, short-notice needs, since its price barely moves.
